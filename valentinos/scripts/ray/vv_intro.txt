RAY: Welcome to Valentino's Vehicles — named in honour of Valentino Braitenberg, whose 1984 book showed how simple wiring between sensors and motors can produce behaviour that looks surprisingly purposeful.

RAY: Each robot you will build here is an analog computer. Sensors read light and proximity. Wires carry those signals — with weights of one, two, or three — to motor inputs.

RAY: The motors respond to whatever signals arrive. A small difference in wiring produces a completely different personality.

RAY: You will observe, you will hypothesise, and you will build. Let's begin.
