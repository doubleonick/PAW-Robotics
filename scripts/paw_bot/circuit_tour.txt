sensors
These are the SENSOR pins. They bring the outside world into the circuit
board — light levels, distances — as electrical signals to work with.

motors
These are the MOTOR pins. Whatever signals you route here drive the wheels.
This is where the circuit's decisions become action.

neurons
Each triangle is a NEURON. It gathers the signals wired into it, combines
them, and produces one output. A neuron changes the "perception" of the
world, which in turn affects the action the connected motors will take.

excitatory
The GREEN (E) inputs are EXCITATORY: signal in pushes the neuron's output
UP. Electrically, they add current that drives the output higher.

inhibitory
The RED (I) inputs are INHIBITORY: signal in pushes the output DOWN.
Electrically, they pull current away, lowering the output.

pots
The knob inside each triangle is a POTENTIOMETER — the neuron's bias.
Negative bias makes excitatory inputs less effective. 
Positive bias makes inhibitory inputs less effective.  

outputs
There are two types of outputs: "N" and "T".  N is a normal output: "fire" 
immediately.  "T" is a threshold output; it tells the neuron, don't 
fire unless the sum of the inputs is positive.  It delays action.
